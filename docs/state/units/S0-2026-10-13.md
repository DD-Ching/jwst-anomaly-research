## A. Wave transmission and echoes of a traversable throat (SV / Ellis–Bronnikov)
1. **Unknown.** How much of a signal at frequency ω and multipole ℓ passes the throat (channel bandwidth;
   propagation). Does a double-peaked SV throat (2M < a < 3M) reflect enough to give ringdown echoes that LVK
   data could limit (existence)?
2. **Ordinary model.** Kerr ringdown with no echoes, plus detector noise and glitches. For bandwidth, a
   free-space aperture of the same radius.
3. **Minimum verification.**
   - Transmission is already published. Do not redo:
     - [withdrawn, see the addendum: arXiv:2606.08351 treats SV black-bounce *black holes*, i.e. horizon transmission, not two-sided throat transmission];
     - Ellis low-energy transmission, 2010.15023;
     - EM/GW suppression below the Ellis–Bronnikov barrier, 2604.14238;
     - SV quasinormal modes and echoes at the BH–WH transition, 1911.11823.
   - The open piece is mapping LVK echo results onto SV parameters. LVK releases only p-values and Bayes
     factors (2112.06861 Table XIV; 2010.14529), with no reflectivity limit. A mapping needs SV echo templates
     injected into strain data and searched with the LVK method. No public template code exists.
   - Estimated cost: well over the 20 h unit cap (GWOSC strain plus a BayesWave-type search).
4. **Continue / stop.** The theory half reproduces published results. The data half does not fit the budget.
   **Not qualified now.**

## B. Far-side mass perturbing orbits on our side (Dai & Stojkovic, arXiv:1910.00429)
1. **Unknown.** Does a mass on the other side of a throat at Sgr A* leave a gravitational imprint on S2's orbit
   (existence; a "gravity channel")?
2. **Ordinary model.** Kepler plus Schwarzschild precession, Rømer delay and redshift, plus extended mass or an
   unseen perturber. GRAVITY allows about 1,200 M☉ inside S2's orbit (arXiv:2409.12261).
3. **Minimum verification.**
   - Data exist: CDS J/ApJ/837/30 (145 astrometric epochs and 44 radial velocities), J/ApJ/854/12 and
     J/ApJ/948/94.
   - The derivation, done here [derived, order of magnitude]:
     - A far-side mass m orbiting at r_p of a few to 30 r_g contributes a static monopole. That is exactly
       degenerate with the central mass M.
     - Its time-varying part has a quadrupole-to-monopole acceleration ratio of (r_p/d)². At S2's pericentre
       (d ≈ 2,830 r_g) that is 1e-6 for r_p = 3, 1.3e-5 for 10 and 1.1e-4 for 30 r_g, even for m = M.
     - That part also oscillates on hours. S2's orbital response to it is suppressed by about
       (P_p/P_S2)² ~ 10⁻¹⁰.
     - S2's relative astrometric precision is about 2e-4.
   - Simonetti et al.'s strongest published limits (2007.12184) used HR 6819 and LB-1, whose black holes were
     later refuted.
4. **Continue / stop.** With S2-like probes, the observable cannot separate a far-side mass from ordinary
   central mass. **Not qualified.**
   - Reopen with a probe at ≲ 100 r_g sampled faster than the far-side period: a pulsar near Sgr A*, or a
     stellar-mass BH–pulsar binary with timing.

## C. Arrival order in strong lensing (a shortcut image arriving before the Fermat minimum)
1. **Unknown.** Does any lensed source show an image that leads the Fermat-potential minimum, or a leading image
   with negative parity (propagation, shortcut)?
2. **Ordinary model.** Smooth lens plus substructure. Substructure is already predicted to cause ordering
   anomalies relative to smooth models (0805.0309; 2405.04779).
3. **Minimum verification.**
   - The theorem itself is settled. In Schneider 1984 (A&A 140, 119), the first image is the global minimum by
     definition, and Σ ≥ 0 is needed only for |μ| ≥ 1.
   - Data: GraL X time-delay compilation CDS J/A+A/707/A345, whose sign convention is undocumented and
     inconsistent. VLBI relative parity exists for B0218+357, PKS 1830-211 and Q0957+561.
   - VLBI gives only relative parity, and the unlensed flux is unknown, so a |μ| ≥ 1 test needs a model.
4. **Continue / stop.** No open unknown that the data can decide beyond the theorem, and substructure mimics an
   ordering anomaly. **Not qualified.**
   - Reopen with a transient source (lensed SN/FRB/GRB) that has model-independent image identification and an
     early, unexplained copy. That overlaps S1/S3's copy searches.

## Addendum 2026-10-13 (owner follow-up review; about 1.4 h more high-risk time)
New inputs, used as the owner instructed: GrayHawk-v2, the public echo code, 2606.08351's scope, and the
Krasnikov comment with the authors' reply.

**A, corrected.**
- **2606.08351 is out of scope as a transmission benchmark.** Lütfüoğlu, "Hawking Emission from Black Holes
  Evaporating toward Wormholes", computes greybody factors of SV **black-bounce black holes** (a < 2M, with a
  horizon) as they approach the wormhole endpoint. That is transmission into a horizon. It is not transmission
  between the two ends of a traversable wormhole, and the earlier line citing it as "SV photon greybody factors"
  for the throat channel is withdrawn.
- **GrayHawk-v2** (Calzà, arXiv:2606.06339; github.com/marcocalza89/GrayHawk-v2 at 919126d, read only, nothing
  run):
  - It is Mathematica notebooks plus a PDF manual, with **no licence file**: a disclaimer only, so all rights are
    reserved by default.
  - It solves the frequency-domain scattering problem for symmetric wormholes (tortoise origin at the throat,
    purely transmitted boundary condition on the far side). Preloaded wormholes: Ellis–Bronnikov, Morris–Thorne,
    Dadhich–Kar–Mukherji–Visser, ZLMY, and Schwarzschild thin shells.
  - Its validation reproduces published transmission figures to residuals of about 1e-4, using raw data that the
    original authors sent privately.
  - **Reusable:** the method, and the benchmark targets (figures of its refs. 114, 135 and 136).
  - **Not reusable:** the code itself. No Wolfram engine is available here, and there is no licence. It also
    gives no time-domain echo series.
- **Public echo code:** none found for wormholes. The LVK and Miani et al. searches publish results, not
  templates.
- **Minimal check, done** [derived; `scripts/x2_echo_delay.py`, `tests/test_x2_echo_delay.py`,
  `results/x2/s0_echo_delay.json`]:
  - For a symmetric SV throat with two potential peaks (2M < a < 3M; for every massless field the peaks sit at
    R = 3M on both sides), the echo spacing is the round trip between the peaks, Δt = 4 ∫₀^{√(9M²−a²)} dr/f.
  - Near a → 2M it grows as a power law, Δt ≈ 2π √(a³/ε) M with ε = 1 − 2M/a. This is **not** the
    logarithmic horizon-scale ECO scaling.
  - Checks: the split integral matches direct quadrature to 1e-8, and the small-ε formula holds to 2 % for
    a − 2M ≤ 1e-4 (0.02 % at 1e-7). At a − 2M ≤ 1e-6, scipy reports round-off warnings in the subtracted
    integrand; there the result still matches the analytic form to ≤ 0.1 %.
  - **Result:** for a 60 M☉ remnant, the 50–350 ms post-merger window searched by Miani et al. (arXiv:2302.12158)
    corresponds to a − 2M ≈ 4.5e-4 to 2.2e-2 M, and the 0.2–1.2 s window to 3.8e-5 to 1.4e-3 M. That needs no
    Planck-scale fine tuning.
  - So the "needs injections beyond the cap" verdict was too strong. Miani et al. publish per-event h_rss
    thresholds at 50 % detection probability (Table I). A first, conservative mapping needs only the SV echo
    amplitude ratio and the events' ringdown h_rss, not new injections.
- **A therefore qualifies** as unit X2 (`docs/state/units/X2.md`), with premises and gates written there.

**B, conditions stated.**
- **Krasnikov (arXiv:1910.07839).** If the perturbation on our side is spherically symmetric and our region is
  vacuum, Birkhoff's theorem makes it Schwarzschild with some mass M*. The ℓ = 0 part is then exactly degenerate
  with the central mass, however accurate the measurement. This is the **static degeneracy** condition. It also
  removes any time dependence of the ℓ = 0 part.
- **Dai & Stojkovic's reply (arXiv:1912.07793).** Their Δa = µ(R/r_p − R/r_a)/r₂² comes from an elliptic,
  non-spherical far-side orbit, which they treat as "a sequence of monopoles". They argue Birkhoff does not apply
  straightforwardly across the throat.
- **Our reading:** a time-varying signal on our side must be carried by ℓ ≥ 1 multipoles crossing the throat.
  Neither paper computes these, and a sequence of monopoles cannot evade Birkhoff for the ℓ = 0 part in our
  vacuum region.
- **The fast-oscillation suppression** used in the screen applies only when the far-side period P_p is much
  shorter than the probe's period: S2 has 16 yr against hours for a far-side orbit at a few to 30 r_g, giving a
  response suppressed by about (P_p/P_S2)².
  - For a far-side body on a **wide** orbit (P_p ~ years), there is no time suppression. The ℓ ≥ 2 field reaching
    our side is instead suppressed by its multipole fall-off through a throat of size R (∝ (R/A)^{ℓ+1}) and
    in our space (∝ (R/d)^{ℓ+2}).
  - The order of magnitude stays far below S2's precision.
- **B stays not qualified.** Its reopen input is unchanged: a probe at ≲ 100 r_g sampled faster than the
  far-side period, or a computed ℓ ≥ 2 transfer through the throat.
