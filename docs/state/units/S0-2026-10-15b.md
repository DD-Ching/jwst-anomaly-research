## 1. What is already known [lit]
- **Kapitza / high-frequency averaging** (Bukov, D'Alessio & Polkovnikov, arXiv:1407.4803v6, §5, Eqs. 92–106;
  Krondorfer et al., arXiv:2507.02736v2, Eqs. 12–39).
  - For a linear mode δ̈ = (γ² + h cos Ωt)δ, the averaged stiffness is h²/(2Ω²) − γ².
  - So stability needs **h > √2 γΩ**, with Ω ≫ γ, and the drive amplitude must grow ∝ Ω. 2507.02736 Fig. 2 shows
    that the averaged boundary fails for strong driving.
- **Floquet SYK wormholes** (arXiv:2404.08394v3) is a coupled-SYK / AdS₂ model.
  - The drive is on the boundary couplings μ(t) and J(t).
  - It heats the system and mostly closes the wormhole. Its only gain is transient, at a/J = 0.0015.
  - None of it transfers to a 4D asymptotically flat throat: the boundary L–R coupling, JT gravity and the
    disorder average are all absent there.
- **Known unstable modes:**
  - Massless Ellis–Bronnikov: exactly one radial unstable mode, growth **β ≈ 1.18 c/r_throat**, potential
    W = −3/(1+x²)² (Gonzalez, Guzman & Sarbach, arXiv:0806.0608v2, §III C and Table I; 2301.05243v2 Table 1 gives
    1.182).
  - 5D equal-J rotation removes the purely imaginary branches at (|J|/A)_cr = 0.10063 (arXiv:1309.2448v1).
  - Our S6a: they continue as an oscillating growing pair, about 0.5 of the static growth (`X2_s6a_summary.md`).
  - In 4D, slow rotation raises ω_I as J²·16.358 (2301.05243, 2403.08387).
- **Topological censorship** (Friedman, Schleich & Witt, gr-qc/9305017v2, Theorem 1): a traversable wormhole in an
  asymptotically flat, globally hyperbolic spacetime needs ANEC to fail on some inextendible null geodesic.
- **Searched and not found:** no paper stabilises a wormhole, or any gravitational instability, by high-frequency
  driving, and none applies Isaacson or Brill–Hartle averaging to wormholes. The queries are in the subagent
  report, summarised in SOURCES.

## 3. Minimal check
### 3a. Where an ordinary drive can enter at all [der]
1. **Spherical drives from outside cannot act.**
   - In spherical symmetry the Misner–Sharp mass inside radius r changes only through matter crossing r.
   - So matter outside the throat region changes the interior only by a rescaling of the clock (lapse): the
     generalised Birkhoff / shell theorem.
   - Hence a spherical external "shaker" has **no** first- or second-order effect on the l = 0 throat mode. The
     drive must sit at the throat or cross it.
   - A wormhole has no single "inside": the statement holds while the drive matter stays outside bounding
     spheres on **both** sides of the throat.
   - In the 5D equal-J sector (S6a) Birkhoff does not hold: the squashing degree of freedom radiates. So this
     no-go is 4D-spherical only.
2. **A non-spherical high-frequency drive acts on the slow throat mode only at second order.**
   - Its first-order effect averages to zero over the fast period.
   - The second-order mean is the Brill–Hartle / Isaacson effective stress tensor, which is GR's counterpart of
     Kapitza's averaged force. Its validity condition (wavelength ≪ curvature scale) is Kapitza's Ω ≫ γ.
     (Standard result: MTW §35.15; Isaacson 1968. Not re-read this round.)
   - For EM or GW waves in the geometric-optics limit this stress is a positive null fluid, which satisfies the
     NEC.
   - **So "dynamic stabilisation by ordinary waves" reduces, under assumptions, to a near-static question:**
     does adding NEC-satisfying radiation to the wormhole make its radial mode stable?
   - The assumptions are **[hyp]**: the averaged radiation responds adiabatically; it is modelled as an
     isotropic gas rather than a directed null fluid; and it exchanges no energy with the exotic matter, each
     component being conserved separately.
3. **A first-order (direct) Kapitza drive would have to be non-perturbative.** For the Ellis mode (γ = 1.18/a)
   with Ω = 10γ, the Kapitza bound needs a stiffness modulation h > √2 γΩ ≈ 20/a². The whole static potential is
   only 3/a² deep, so the drive would be about 6–7 times larger than it, outside the linear mode picture.
   (Single-mode projection; how the drive enters W is assumed linear.)

### 3b. Thin-shell test of the reduced question [der] for the formula, [num] for the table (`scripts/s0_kapitza_shell.py` → `results/s0/kapitza_shell.json`)
**Model:** an exact thin-shell Schwarzschild wormhole (Israel junction conditions; Poisson & Visser 1995). The
throat radius a(t) is an explicit degree of freedom. The shell carries:
- an exotic component with a linear EoS slope β²_x;
- a 2D radiation component (p = σ/2, σ_r ∝ a⁻³), standing for the averaged confined drive.

The throat a₀ is fixed, the radiation share is f = σ_r/|σ₀|, and the exotic part absorbs the difference.

**Checks:** the static throat satisfies V = V′ = 0 to ≤ 1e-6, and the β² slope of V″ is Poisson–Visser's,
−(4/a₀²)(1 − 3M/a₀) (tests).

**Result:** the total stiffness is β²_eff = β²_x + (½ − β²_x)·S_r/S₀, where S = σ + p.
- Above 3M the shell has S₀ < 0 (NEC violated). Radiation then pushes β²_eff away from ½.
  - It **stabilises** when β²_x < ½.
  - It **destabilises** when β²_x > ½ (by the formula; tested at 0.75 and 1.0).

| a₀/M | β²_x | radiation share needed, f | exotic negative energy ×(1 + f) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0 / 0.25 | 0.58 / 1.33 | 1.58 / 2.33 |
| 6 | 0 / 0.25 | 0.44 / 1.13 | 1.44 / 2.13 |
| 10 | 0 / 0.25 | 0.38 / 1.05 | 1.38 / 2.05 |
| 20 | 0 / 0.25 | 0.35 / 1.02 | 1.35 / 2.02 |
| 2.5 (S₀ > 0) | 0 / 0.25 | 2.33 / 4.33 | 3.33 / 5.33 |
| any tested | 0.75, 1.0 | never (radiation destabilises) | — |

**Reading:**
- In this toy, a confined positive-energy drive **can** supply a restoring effect: no sign no-go.
- But it needs radiation energy of the same order as the throat's exotic energy, f ≈ 0.35–1.3, and it raises the
  exotic negative energy by 35–130 %.
- The thin shell's exotic EoS is a free function, so the same stabilisation could be had by choosing β² directly
  (Poisson–Visser). The toy therefore shows the sign is allowed. It does not show that a real field-supported
  wormhole can be stabilised.

### 3c. Scale of the drive [der], order of magnitude
- **Frequency:** Ω ≫ c/a, e.g. ≫ 3 × 10⁵ s⁻¹ for a = 1 km. Radio or optical drives satisfy this easily, so
  frequency is not the obstacle.
- **Energy:** the radiation energy is E_r = 2f a √(1 − 2M/a)·c⁴/G. For a = 1 km at a₀ = 4M that is about 1 × 10⁴⁷ J,
  about 0.56 M☉c².
- **Power if it is not confined** (leaving on the light-crossing time a/c **[hyp]**): P ≈ 2f √(1 − 2M/a)·c⁵/G
  ≈ (0.7–2)·c⁵/G ≈ **2–8 × 10⁵² W**, whatever the throat size. That is about 10³ times the peak GW luminosity of
  GW150914 (about 3.6 × 10⁴⁹ W).
- **Confinement** at the throat is a new assumption **[hyp]**. The throat's photon sphere is unstable, and
  self-gravitating radiation geons are not known to be stable.

## 4. Can it act on our unstable modes?
- **Ellis l = 0 (4D):** yes in principle, through the averaged stress at the throat (3a.2). Whether it stabilises
  for a field-supported wormhole, where the exotic EoS is fixed by the phantom Lagrangian, is **open**. That is
  the next calculation.
- **S6a 5D oscillating pair:** not computed.
  - It needs a radiation fluid added to the 5D equal-J background, and new perturbation equations.
  - Its oscillating (Krein-collision) instability may respond differently from a purely imaginary mode.
  - Any result would still be 5D only. A 4D statement needs a 4D rotating background (1409.1503) and a stability
    analysis beyond second order in rotation.

## 5. Link to the communication goal (kept separate)
Even if a throat mode were stabilised:
1. **Traversable?** Thin-shell and Ellis throats are traversable in principle. But the stabilising radiation sits
   in the throat at an energy density of the throat-curvature scale, about c⁴/(8πG a²) ≈ 5 × 10³⁶ J/m³ for
   a = 1 km. Any signal crossing it meets that bath.
2. **A shorter path in the same exterior spacetime?** Not addressed. These solutions join two asymptotic regions.
   A same-universe shortcut between points 20–100 ly apart needs a separate global construction.
3. **Can ordinary matter make or control it?** No. The drive only modifies stability. Exotic (ANEC-violating)
   support is still required (topological censorship), and here it **grows** by 35–130 %. The drive does not
   presuppose a channel, but it cannot create one either.

"A mode no longer grows" ≠ "can communicate".
