## A. Wave-optics lensing of gravitational waves by negative-mass (and Ellis) compact objects
1. **Unknown.**
   - A negative-mass point lens (n = 1, ε < 0 in the D-047 family) acts on a GW signal through its
     frequency-dependent amplification factor F(f). An Ellis wormhole is a different lens (zero mass, n = 2). It is
     treated as a separate model in X4 S1; nothing here applies to it.
   - LVK's searches for single distorted signals fit only ordinary lenses:
     - O3: point mass and SIS (arXiv:2304.08393);
     - O4a: an isolated point mass (GWTC-4.0 lensing, arXiv:2512.16347).
   - The published follow-ups of the one O4a outlier, GW231123_135430, use positive-mass lenses: an embedded or
     binary microlens (arXiv:2512.17631, 2512.19118) or an isolated diffracting lens (arXiv:2607.21834).
     arXiv:2604.08179 argues that unmodelled lensing could mimic a massive-graviton signature.
   - No GW-band wave-optics treatment of a repulsive lens was found. The closest are Yoo, Harada & Tsukamoto
     arXiv:1302.7170 (EM femtolensing by an Ellis wormhole) and the "nothing" entry W6 in `docs/exotic_lensing.md`.
   - The missing answer: is a negative-mass lens's GW signature distinguishable from ordinary lensing, from
     unlensed signals and from waveform systematics, and at what SNR?
   - Bearing on the goal: negative mass is the exotic-matter ingredient of traversable wormholes. GWs probe
     cosmological compact lenses of 10–10⁴ M☉, a population and scale X3 cannot reach.
2. **Against explicit ordinary models.**
   - Positive point mass, done here.
   - Positive embedded or binary lenses, SIS; not done.
   - Unlensed signals with free intrinsic parameters (masses, spins, precession, higher modes), detector
     calibration, glitches; not done, the key open question.
3. **Minimal verification, done** (`scripts/s0_gw_neglens.py`, `tests/test_s0_gw_neglens.py`,
   `results/s0/gw_neglens.json`; derived and simulated, no strain data):
   - **Closed form.** For a point lens of sign s:
     F_s(w, y) = exp[sπw/4 + i(sw/2) ln(w/2)] Γ(1 − isw/2) ₁F₁(isw/2; 1; iwy²/2).
     It matches direct numerical integration of the diffraction integral (contour rotated by π/4): to 1e-9 for
     s = −1 and ≤ 3e-7 for s = +1 at the tested points (w ≤ 5; test tolerance 1e-5). The rotation is valid for
     both signs. It tends to 1 as w → 0. At u = 3 its fringe-averaged geometric limit is the W3 magnification of
     `exotic_sim` to < 3 %.
   - **Physics.** A negative-mass lens is a **low-pass filter**: for y < 2 the high frequencies fall into the
     wave-optics version of the W3 dip (|F| = 0.036 at w = 5, y = 1). A positive mass amplifies instead.
   - **Distinguishability.**
     - Setup: noise-weighted overlap; amplitude, phase and time free; intrinsic parameters fixed;
       inspiral-only |h| ∝ f^(−7/6) up to f_ISCO; an aLIGO design-curve fit (attributed to Ajith 2011, not
       checked against the paper body) or a flat PSD as a check;
       binaries of 20 and 60 M☉.
     - With the amplitude free, the best-fit residual is Δχ² = ρ²(1 − O²), where ρ is the **observed (lensed)**
       SNR. So ρ_req = 3/√(1 − O²) for Δχ² = 9. (The first version used 2ρ²(1 − O), which holds only as O → 1;
       fixed after review.)
     - Required SNR to separate a negative lens from the best positive point mass (36 × 30 grid):
       - lens mass M_Lz = 300–1,000 M☉ with y ≤ 1: ρ_req ≈ 3.1–16.5 (aLIGO);
       - the same masses at y = 1.5: ρ_req ≈ 4.8–46.5;
       - M_Lz = 100 M☉ with y ≤ 1: ρ ≈ 8–61;
       - M_Lz = 30 M☉, or y = 2.5: ρ ≈ 30–770, not distinguishable at LVK SNRs.
     - With the flat PSD: ρ_req ≈ 4.9–19.5 for M_Lz = 300–1,000 M☉ with y ≤ 1, the same conclusion.
     - Against unlensed signals the required SNRs are similar or lower, except at M_Lz = 30 M☉, y = 2.5
       (152 vs 107 aLIGO; 123 vs 84 flat).
     - **Selection cost.** The separable cases are strongly demagnified: |F| ≤ 0.07–0.18 across the band for
       M_Lz = 1,000 M☉, y ≤ 1. A binary must therefore be intrinsically much louder to reach a given observed ρ,
       and the detectable volume for such configurations falls by ≳ 100×. This decides which configurations are
       plausible, and S1 must weight by it.
     - **These are optimistic** (intrinsic parameters fixed, inspiral only). Some best positive fits sit at the
       grid edge, so a wider grid could only lower the mismatch.
4. **Continue / stop.**
   - **Qualifies** as unit X4. The unknown is concrete, the data are public (GWOSC strain), and an open
     real-data case exists (GW231123).
   - The decisive next question is whether intrinsic-parameter freedom absorbs the low-pass signature, since a
     high-frequency cut also mimics a heavier binary. That needs an IMR waveform model, so it is X4 S1, not done
     here.
   - **Stop** if, with intrinsic parameters free, a negative lens is matched by an unlensed or ordinary-lens
     signal to 1 − FF² < 9/ρ² at ρ ≤ 30 for every counted configuration (X4 S1 gate).

## B. Vanishing point sources on POSS-I plates (VASCO)
1. **Unknown.** Are the single-epoch POSS-I point sources real astrophysical transients or emulsion flaws?
   - Solano et al. 2022 catalogue: 5,399 candidates.
   - Hambly & Blair 2024: profiles narrower and rounder than stars, read as flaws.
   - Villarroel et al. reply: sub-second flashes would look like that.
   - arXiv:2507.15896 (image profiles), arXiv:2604.04810 (independent recovery pipeline).
   - **No mechanism links a vanishing point source to a wormhole or channel.** No model found predicts one.
2. **Ordinary models:** emulsion flaws, plate artefacts, flare stars, solar-system objects, reflections. These
   dominate the published debate.
3. **Minimal step:** plate-image tools (we have none) and adjudication of a contested artefact question.
4. **Not qualified.** No prediction ties it to the hypothesis, so a resolution in either direction would not
   change our judgement on exotic channels. **Reopen if** a physical model predicts vanishing or appearing point
   sources with a signature distinct from flares and flaws.

## C. Reanalysis of the quantum-processor "traversable wormhole" experiment
1. **Unknown.** Does the 9-qubit Sycamore signal of Jafferis et al., Nature 612, 51 (2022), with its learned sparse
   SYK Hamiltonian, show gravitational features (size winding) beyond generic teleportation?
   - Kobrin, Schuster & Yao, "Comment on 'Traversable wormhole dynamics on a quantum processor'"
     (arXiv:2302.07897), argue that the learned Hamiltonian does not thermalize and matches SYK teleportation
     only for the training operators. Jafferis et al. replied.
   - A philosophical essay discusses the experiment (PhilSci-Archive 22120, arXiv:2301.03522).
2. **Ordinary model:** standard quantum teleportation and scrambling in a small spin system. Even a positive
   answer is a quantum simulation of a holographic toy model, not a spacetime channel. It needs a classical
   channel and carries nothing faster than light. Under T5 a simulation is never observational evidence.
3. **Minimal step:** reproduce the published Hamiltonian's size-winding diagnostics. Feasible, but its outcome
   is a statement about a 7-fermion model.
4. **Not qualified** for this trial's goal (real anomalies or mechanisms in nature). **Reopen if** a proposal
   makes such an experiment predict something about real spacetime.
