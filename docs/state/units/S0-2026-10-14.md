## A. Pulsar-timing sign test: a Shapiro *advance* from a negative-mass or exotic lens
1. **Unknown.** Do compact objects with negative or exotic mass exist in the Galaxy (existence)? A pulse whose
   line of sight passes such an object at impact parameter b is advanced, not delayed. For the D-047 lens family
   α = ε/bⁿ, the time-variable excess delay is:
   - Δt = −(ε/c) ln b for n = 1;
   - Δt = (ε/c) b^{1−n}/(n−1) for n > 1.

   [derived from the lens potential]

   So the sign of a timing bump measures the sign of ε, and its time profile measures n. Ordinary matter always
   gives a delay with n = 1.
2. **Ordinary models.** A positive-mass flyby (n = 1, ε > 0), red spin noise, chromatic dispersion-measure and
   scattering events (removed by multi-frequency TOAs), glitches, and solar-wind residuals.
3. **Minimum verification: the reach**, done here [derived, order of magnitude; `scripts/s0_pta_shapiro.py` →
   `results/s0/pta_shapiro_reach.json`].
   - Assumptions: a NANOGrav 15-year-like array (68 pulsars, 15 yr, 1 kpc paths), a 0.3 µs bump threshold after
     the spin fit, half the bump absorbed by that fit, 200 km/s transverse speed, and local ρ_DM = 0.01 M☉ pc⁻³.
   - **Point mass of either sign.** The expected number of events, even if such objects made up all the dark
     matter, peaks at about 0.08 near |M| ≈ 0.03 M☉. Three events would need 36× the local dark-matter density.
     The sign test cannot reach any physically allowed abundance.
     - Allowing a 10× better threshold and 2× the span (N ∝ T²), the peak rises only to order 1. That is still
       below 3 events for an all-dark-matter population.
   - **Ellis throats (n = 2, massless).** Δt = πa²/(4cb), about 25 µs for a = 10⁵ km at b = 7 AU. A null result
     would give n₉₅ ≈ 9 pc⁻³ (a = 10⁵ km) to 2 pc⁻³ (a ≥ 10⁶ km).
     - Such densities imply a bulge microlensing optical depth of order n π R_E² L. For a = 10⁵ km,
       R_E ≈ (πa²D/4)^{1/3} ≈ 5 AU, which gives τ ≈ 10⁻⁵ at n = 1 pc⁻³.
     - That is above the observed bulge τ ≈ 2 × 10⁻⁶ [order of magnitude], and the OGLE-IV E2pos fits found
       nothing (D-057).
     - So pulsar timing would only re-limit densities that microlensing already excludes.
4. **Continue / stop.** **Not qualified.** The observable is right in principle (sign and shape), but no
   abundance it can reach is still open. The data (public PTA releases) are not the bottleneck.
   - Reopen with an SKA-era array (≥ 10× more pulsars at ≤ 50 ns over ≥ 20 yr), or a model that predicts
     exotic objects clustered along pulsar lines of sight.

## B. FRB lensing by Ellis or negative-mass lenses
1. **Unknown.** Do cosmological Ellis or negative-mass lenses of 10⁻³–10³ M☉-equivalent scale exist (existence)?
   They would produce delayed copies at microsecond to second delays.
2. **Ordinary model.** A point-mass (stellar, PBH) lens, plus intrinsic sub-bursts and scintillation.
   - In geometric optics, a single delayed copy (Δt, flux ratio R) is fitted equally well by a point mass, an
     Ellis lens or a negative mass, because each has one free scale. The Ellis case is shown in 1302.7170 ("almost
     indistinguishable"). For the negative-mass lens, our own derivation (not checked against the literature)
     gives the leading image as the brighter one, as for a point mass.
   - Only the population distribution of R at a given Δt, or wave-optics effects, separates the lens types.
3. **Minimum verification.**
   - No FRB-lensing paper treats Ellis or negative-mass lenses. The searches checked are 2204.06001/06014,
     2109.09251, 2112.03721, 2206.14310, 2002.12533 and 1605.00008.
   - A reinterpretation of public-catalogue null results in the style of Takahashi & Asada (1303.1301) would be
     new and cheap.
   - Baseband data are on CANFAR, which was unreachable from here (404/503), as already noted for S1.
4. **Continue / stop.** **Not qualified** as an evidence search: an echo, if found, could not be told apart from an
   ordinary lens (item 2).
   - An Ellis/negative-mass abundance bound from catalogue nulls stays a possible low-priority limit task. It is
     recorded here and not started.
   - Reopen if wave-optics FRB lensing data (baseband, many bursts) become reachable, or a lensed-FRB candidate shows
     an R–Δt combination that point masses cannot produce.

## C. W3 inverted microlensing in *unselected* Gaia epoch photometry
1. **Unknown.** Do negative-mass compact lenses exist outside the bulge fields (existence)?
   - D-061 found that the Gaia DR3 microlensing selection rejects W3 events. The question is whether a screen
     applied **before any variability selection** can recover them at Gaia's cadence and reject ordinary dimmers.
   - The signature (D-047) is an achromatic dip toward the blend level lasting 2t_E√(4 − u₀²), flanked by caustic
     brightenings.
2. **Ordinary models**, over their plausible ranges:
   - eclipsing binaries: periodic dips, nearly achromatic;
   - YSO dippers and R CrB stars: dust dimming, so reddening (chromatic);
   - long-period and semi-regular variables;
   - positive-mass microlensing (brightening);
   - Gaia transit-level outliers, crowding and scan-angle photometry effects.

   Gaia's per-epoch G with BP/RP gives the achromaticity test, and the 34-month (DR3) or 66-month (DR4) span gives
   the single-versus-periodic test.
3. **Minimum verification.**
   - **Data exist now** [observed, Gaia archive TAP 2026-10-14]: the Gaia Andromeda Photometric Survey (GAPS) in DR3
     publishes G/BP/RP epoch photometry for **all 1,257,319 sources** in 5.5° around M31 (`in_andromeda_survey`;
     all have `has_epoch_photometry`). They are not selected by variability: the sources checked have
     `phot_variable_flag` = NOT_AVAILABLE.
   - Known ordinary dimmers with DR3 epoch photometry come from the DR3 variability classes.
   - Tools exist: `jwst_anomaly.gaia_mulens` (DataLink epoch photometry), `scripts/w3_gaia.py` and the D-057 fitter
     with exotic models, and `exotic_sim`.
   - DR4 (66 months) is scheduled for 2 December 2026 (ESA "Gaia data release scenario" page, read 2026-10-14). The
     DR4 page does not yet state whether epoch photometry will cover all sources: an assumption until confirmed.
4. **Continue / stop.**
   - **Qualified** as unit X3.
   - **Continue** if, on real unselected Gaia light curves, injected W3 events are recovered with a useful
     efficiency, and ordinary sources and known dimmers give a low enough false-alarm rate. The thresholds are
     frozen in `docs/state/units/X3.md` before any search.
   - **Stop** if Gaia's sparse cadence cannot separate W3 from ordinary dimmers at any useful efficiency. Gaia then
     cannot test W3, and D-061's DR4 "revisit" closes with a measured reason.
   - **Expected yield.** The GAPS line of sight (b ≈ −21°) has a tiny Galactic optical depth, so a GAPS search is
     a validation run and a null limit, not a likely discovery. The decision-changing data set is DR4.
