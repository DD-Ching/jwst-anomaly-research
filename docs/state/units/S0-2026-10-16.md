# S0 check 2026-10-16: a finite message-and-reply window in an unstable wormhole

Owner request 2026-10-16. Literature and reusable-tool check, ≤ 2 h, started 00:30 UTC. No solver was built, no
evolution was run and no published result was rescanned. X4/R1 and S6a are unchanged. The S0-2026-10-15b
static radiation-fluid eigenvalue computation is cancelled (owner accepted #256).

**Question:** in a wormhole model that exists but is unstable, is there a finite time window long enough to send an
identifiable message and receive a reply? How far does the literature actually prove this?

**Labels:**
- **[lit]** published, read in the paper body;
- **[ours]** earlier results of this project;
- **[der]** order-of-magnitude reasoning in this round, not computed;
- **[open]** not answered anywhere we found.

## 1. Published results [lit]
All of these papers use the same model class:
- the Ellis–Bronnikov (Morris–Thorne drainhole) wormhole in 4D;
- spherical symmetry;
- a massless ghost (phantom) scalar as the source;
- perturbation by self-gravitating scalar pulses.

Each joins **two separate asymptotically flat universes.**

1. **Shinkai & Hayward, gr-qc/0205041v2** (PRD 66, 044005, 2002). They use a dual-null code covering both universes.
   - **Outcomes:**
     - If the pulse adds net negative energy (E₀ < 0), the throat inflates (H ≈ 1.1–1.56/a).
     - If it adds net positive energy (E₀ > 0), the throat collapses to a black hole with M → about 0.30 a. A normal-matter pulse always does this.
   - **Time to the horizon:** e^(x⁻_H − 3) ∝ E₀^(−0.60), in null coordinates, units of a. So the delay grows only logarithmically as the perturbation shrinks.
   - **Traversal:** a fast traveller "may go through … and exit safely into the other universe". This is shown qualitatively from the Penrose diagram; no geodesics are traced.
   - **Maintenance (§III D, Fig. 11):** a traveller pulse followed by a ghost pulse with c_a = 0.02385 keeps the throat "at least until x⁻ = 10" (the point where the final structure was judged); c_a = 0.02390 inflates it instead. The authors say the fine-tuning "may not be realistic", and that continual adjustment "would be a never-ending project".
   - The paper assumed linear stability. Later work showed that assumption was wrong (item 2).
2. **González, Guzmán & Sarbach, arXiv:0806.0608v2 (CQG 26, 015010) and 0806.1370v2 (CQG 26, 015011), 2009.**
   - **Instability:** there is exactly one unstable mode under linear spherical perturbations, with e-folding time 0.846 r_throat/c for the massless case. In the nonlinear evolution, small perturbations follow this rate; larger ones collapse faster.
   - **Round trip (0806.1370, Fig. 4, "Path of trek", "Mirror"):** a **possible test-photon** path, drawn by the authors with a "possible mirror point", goes from one universe to the other, is reflected, and reaches the asymptotic region of the home universe before the black hole forms.
   - **Conclusions:**
     - the collapse time is "of the order of … a few microseconds for a throat with areal radius of one kilometer";
     - a returning observer "cannot explore an arbitrarily large region of the other universe".
3. **Doroshkevich, Hansen, Novikov & Shatskiy, arXiv:0812.0702v2** (IJMPD 18, 1665, 2009). This is a self-gravitating scalar shell.
   - The shell passes through before the collapse begins. Afterwards, passage "is impossible in any direction".
   - For a weak signal, the time between passage and collapse "can be very long". Fig. 6(b) plots the collapse time against the amplitude A_Φ; no scaling law is fitted, and we did not read values off the plot.
4. **Calhoun, Fay & Kain, arXiv:2210.04905v2** (PRD 106, 104054, 2022). This is a new 3+1 code; it is not named and its availability is not stated.
   - **§V, Fig. 3 (A₁ = 0):** a self-gravitating normal-matter probe crosses (amplitude 0.002/r₀). It emits **test null rays** after crossing. Some rays pass back through the throat and reach r = 5 r₀ (the edge of the plot); some "get caught inside the black hole".
   - **Read by eye from Fig. 3, not stated by the authors** (coordinate time under the slicing of Eq. 38, so gauge-dependent; not an observer's proper time):
     - the probe crosses the throat at t ≈ 3 r₀/c;
     - an apparent horizon appears at t ≈ 6;
     - the last escaping ray crosses the throat at t ≈ 10.
   - **§VI, Fig. 7:** the massive asymmetric case gives the same picture for A₁ = 0.1 only, with a different slicing (Eq. 39).
   - **Not computed:** the outbound message is the matter pulse itself, so there is no light-signal exchange in both directions. There is also no redshift, attenuation, signal energy or receiver worldline.
5. **Xu, Chew & Yeom, arXiv:2503.07610v1** (2025). This is a double-null code; it is not named and its availability is not stated.
   - Normal pulses lead to collapse and phantom pulses to inflation, as above.
   - **Fig. 7:** an outgoing **phantom** pulse timed to collide with an ingoing normal pulse "does not stabilize the wormhole; it just temporarily extends the traversability … by delaying singularity formation" (M = 0).
   - **Fig. 8:** for M = 0.1, it "temporarily sustains traversability".
   - **No durations are given.** Traversability is judged only from the apparent horizons; no geodesics are traced.
   - **Internal inconsistencies:**
     - the Fig. 9 caption and the text disagree on whether the case is u = 7–8 or 8–9;
     - App. A reads a convergence ratio of about 2 as second order;
     - §II.D swaps the field labels.

Other searches:
- Shirokov, arXiv:2604.00071 (3D GRTeclyn, phantom bounce) and Koyama & Hayward (gr-qc/0406113) and Hayward & Koyama (gr-qc/0406080), impulsive constructions, were read in the abstract only. They do not compute traversal windows.
- No paper was found that quantifies the information content of such a window.

**[der] Window scaling.** This is not a result: it is linear theory extrapolated beyond the published runs.
- The mode grows from an initial amplitude ε until it is nonlinear. That takes about 0.846 ln(1/ε) r_throat/c, plus a few r_throat/c of collapse.
- Example: going from ε = 10⁻³ to ε = 10⁻³⁰ adds only about 53 r_throat/c. For r_throat = 1 km that is about 0.2 ms.
- The message, the reply and any ambient perturbation each raise ε. So the window can be long only for a large throat with a very quiet throat region. No paper fixes the noise floor ε; it is [open].
- SH02's fit, e^(x⁻_H − 3) ∝ E₀^(−0.60), is in null coordinates and in energy rather than amplitude. It was not converted to the 0.846 e-folding time here.

## 4. Background phantom versus active phantom maintenance
- **The background needs phantom.** Every model above is supported by a ghost scalar. It supplies the null-energy violation that topological censorship requires (FSW, gr-qc/9305017; cited from our earlier record, not re-read this round). That is a premise of the model, not a device.
- **Every published maintenance scheme we found uses active phantom emission.**
  - SH02 §III D and XCY25 Figs. 7–8 extend traversability by **injecting further phantom pulses** with tuned amplitude and timing. In SH02, the kept case and the inflating case differ by 0.2 % in c_a (our arithmetic, not a measured tolerance). Neither paper shows that injection is *necessary*. XCY25 also reports that a larger wormhole mass delays collapse without injection; that is a model parameter, not a control.
  - This is not a control method available to ordinary equipment. It presupposes a source that emits negative-energy fields on demand.
  - It is also not stabilisation:
    - SH02 says "at least until x⁻ = 10" (the judgement point);
    - XCY25 says "temporarily", with no duration.
- **None of the following is stability or reliable communication:**
  - a short delay of the collapse;
  - re-coincidence of the apparent horizons (XCY25 Fig. 9);
  - one test ray getting through.

## 5. The two ends
- Every model here joins **two distinct asymptotically flat universes**. There is no mouth pair in one exterior, and no external reference light path between the mouths.
- So **no delay advantage over a 20–100 ly external path can be stated.** The comparison is undefined in these geometries.
- What remains is conditional only: in a two-universe Ellis wormhole perturbed by a weak ordinary pulse, a test photon can pass back, or follow a possible round-trip path, within a window of order r_throat/c, growing like log(1/ε) [lit + der].
- A dynamical same-exterior two-mouth evolution was not looked for in this round. Its existence is [open].

## 7. Verdict: stop, no new unit
- **What the literature proves** [lit]:
  - in an Ellis wormhole that is assumed to exist (phantom-supported) and is unstable, a self-gravitating ordinary pulse can cross;
  - a **test** light ray can then return, or make a full round trip, before the black hole forms;
  - the window is of order r_throat/c [lit + der], extended only logarithmically by weaker perturbations, or temporarily by fine-tuned active phantom injection;
  - all of this is in a two-universe geometry.
  - This is essentially the result "assume the wormhole and phantom matter exist, then light gets through", with the instability time added.
- **Real gaps** [open]:
  - a finite-energy reply and its back-reaction;
  - redshift at a receiver worldline;
  - message capacity;
  - the noise floor ε;
  - a same-exterior geometry.
- **Why no next step is recommended.** The one computable gap is a two-leg self-gravitating exchange, with redshift on a fixed-radius worldline, using `wh_dual` or a reimplementation. It is not recommended because its plausible outcomes do not change the feasibility judgement:
  - [der] The window is expected to come out of order r_throat/c, reduced by the reply's own energy. That is already implied by SH02's E₀ scaling and by GGS09's linear rate.
  - The judgement is set by three things such a run cannot change:
    - phantom support is a premise;
    - every published maintenance scheme uses active phantom emission, which ordinary equipment cannot supply;
    - there is no same-exterior reference path.
  - The run would also require permission to use unlicensed code, or a new solver. This round excludes a new solver.
- **Reopen if any of these appears:**
  1. a published dynamical evolution of two mouths in **one** exterior, with a reference path;
  2. a wormhole that keeps a window ≫ r_throat/c under ordinary (positive-energy) perturbations, without phantom injection;
  3. a published finite-energy round-trip computation reporting a window or a redshift that contradicts the order-r_throat/c scaling.

## Addendum 2026-10-16 (owner, on accepting the pause)
- **What "stop" means.** The stop means this question is not worth further investment now. It does **not** mean the
  literature has solved the full communication problem. Every [open] item in §3 stays open.
- **The linear growth time is not an upper bound.** The 0.846 r_throat/c e-folding time, and the §3 [der] window
  scaling built on it, are order-of-magnitude scales. They are not a strict upper bound on the usable communication
  window at a receiver.
