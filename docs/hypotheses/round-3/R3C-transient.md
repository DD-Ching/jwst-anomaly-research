# R3-C — "Resonant Partner Density": transient connectivity as a density-of-states factor in clock-depth space

*System A, round 3, group R3-C. Everything below is a hypothesis. No literature was consulted for this round. Every numerical input comes from memory and is labelled as an estimate.*

## 0. One-paragraph statement

Connectivity does not join places. It joins **states**. A macroscopic runaway, the trigger of an irreversible collective event, can only complete if the region that is about to run away forms a link to some "partner" element of record-forming matter whose proper-time rate relative to the cosmic age function matches its own. The rate of such events is then proportional to the **density of resonant partners** in clock-depth space. This is the role a density of final states plays in a golden-rule rate. Because the partner population is the whole baryonic content of the event's causal past, a single partner contributes about 10⁻⁶⁰ of the total. As a result there are no event–event correlations, while the rate still depends weakly and smoothly on the local clock depth λ. The predicted signature is a suppression of supernova rates per unit stellar mass, at fixed host properties, in deeper external gravitational potentials. Its derived slope is d ln Γ / dλ ≈ −8×10³, uncertain by a factor of 3. Event properties should show no accompanying shift.

## 1. Axioms

- **A1 (records).** The primitive is a growing set of *records*: irreversible, coarse-grained distinctions. Space, time and matter are not assumed. A record has predecessors. The **age** of a record is the length of the longest predecessor chain back to the initial boundary (the set of records with no predecessors).
- **A2 (emergent geometry).** In the continuum limit, chain length becomes the maximal proper time from the initial boundary. This defines a scalar field τ_max(x), the "age function", which is fixed by the geometry and the boundary and not by any vacuum vector field. Along a coarse-grained matter worldline with 4-velocity u, the **clock depth** is λ ≡ 1 − (dτ/dτ_max). In the weak-field, slow-motion limit about a homogeneous background, λ ≈ −Φ/c² + v²/2c². Here Φ is the Newtonian-gauge potential and v is the velocity relative to ∇τ_max, which is the cosmic (CMB) frame.
- **A3 (connectivity).** A *collective runaway* is an event in which a macroscopic configuration crosses an instability threshold: thermonuclear ignition of a degenerate core, core bounce, a magnetic-topology change. It completes only if the runaway frontier forms a **link** to a partner record. Link attempts are Poisson-distributed and democratic: the probability per unit partner measure is uniform. A link forms only to a partner with matching clock depth, within a resonance kernel K_w(λ−λ′), taken as Gaussian of width w.
- **A4 (resolution).** The matching resolution w equals the intrinsic fractional fluctuation of chain-length counting. This is identified with the same graininess that appears as the primordial curvature amplitude: **w ≡ A_s^{1/2} ≈ 4.6×10⁻⁵**, with A_s ≈ 2.1×10⁻⁹ (Planck-era value, from memory). If the relevant variable is Φ rather than ζ, w = (3/5)A_s^{1/2}. This factor of 1.7 is carried as uncertainty.
- **A5 (scope).** Single quantum transitions (decays, individual reactions, photon emission and absorption) are not collective runaways. They form records but do not need links. Continuous unitary evolution and propagation form no records at all.
- **A6 (partners).** The partners are record-forming baryonic matter in the event's causal past. Dark matter forms no records and is excluded.

**Honest resemblances.** Causal-set sprinkling, where longest chain equals proper time. Cosmological time functions in GR. Khronometric or aether theories, which have a preferred foliation, though here it is derived from τ_max and not postulated. Machian dependence of local physics on the cosmic matter distribution. Fermi's golden rule (density of final states). Screened scalar fields such as chameleon and symmetron models (environment-dependent effects). Claims of radioactive-decay modulation, which A5 explicitly excludes from the effect.

## 2. Lorentz invariance and the bounds

- The laws use only scalars: τ_max (geometry plus boundary), λ (built from τ_max and the matter 4-velocity), and the partner measure, which is a count of records and therefore invariant. No vacuum vector field exists. The cosmic frame shows up only as the gradient of τ_max, in the same way the CMB shows up: as a feature of the state, not of the laws.
- **Propagation is untouched.** Photons and gravitational waves in transit form no records (A5), so no link factor ever enters a dispersion relation. As a result c_gw = c_γ = c exactly. The 10⁻¹⁵ (GW170817) and ~10⁻¹⁸ (laboratory isotropy) bounds constrain only propagation and continuous dynamics, and the model makes no change there.
- **Where the risk sits (lesson 5).** The preferred-frame content is real but confined to the *rates of collective runaways*, through the v²/2c² term in λ. It is suppressed by the derived slope and not by an O(1) coefficient. The laboratory consequence is an **annual modulation of macroscopic threshold-nucleation rates**. The annual change in λ is Δλ ≈ v_☉ v_⊕ cos θ / c² ≈ 1.2×10⁻⁷. For an event at bulk clock depth the slope is |K| ≲ 10⁴ (see §4). The resulting amplitude is ≲ 10⁻³, with phase fixed by the CMB dipole (extrema near early March and early September). This is not excluded by any test I know of. Superheated-liquid nucleation and dielectric-breakdown statistics could reach it. It is a genuine extra falsifier, and the model does not hide it behind an exact zero.
- No fifth force and no equivalence-principle violation: links move no momentum and no energy. Only the *timing* of the release of stored energy changes. Eötvös-type bounds (~10⁻¹⁵) are untouched.

## 3. Recovery of standard physics, and why the coincidence nulls hold

- **Standard physics** is everything outside A3. Collective-runaway rates become Γ = Γ₀ · S_w(λ)/⟨S_w⟩, where Γ₀ is the usual astrophysical rate. Every rate calibration astronomers use absorbs the normalisation ⟨S_w⟩. Only *differential* dependence on λ is new.
- **No shortcuts.** Nothing travels through a link. A link is a matching condition and not a channel, so no distances shrink, there is no extra lensing, and there is no dimming (lesson 3: there is no dimming of any colour).
- **Coincidence nulls.** The partner measure for a typical event is about 10⁵⁰–10⁶⁰ baryon-scale records within the resonance window. One distant event changes S_w at some other site by about one part in that number. So the induced covariance between any two events is about 10⁻⁵⁰ or smaller, far below the burst-twin (< 7×10⁻³) and lagged-coincidence (≲ 10⁻³ per event, ≤ 100 s) limits. Partners are also selected by clock depth, not by time. Matched-λ regions sit at unrelated cosmic times and sky positions, so nothing produces aligned light curves or arrival-time lags.
- **No-signalling.** In principle an agent can change S_w elsewhere by moving mass m, but only by a fraction of order m/M_window ~ 10⁻⁵⁰. This is listed as a weak point (§7) and not hidden.

## 4. The derived modulation

**Rate law.** Combining A3, A4 and A6 gives

  Γ(λ) = Γ₀ · S_w(λ) / ⟨S_w⟩,  S_w(λ) = ∫ dλ′ ρ_b(λ′) K_w(λ − λ′),

where ρ_b(λ) is the baryonic record mass per unit clock depth in the causal past. For small external changes Δλ,

  **δ ln Γ = K · Δλ,  K ≡ d ln S_w / dλ evaluated at λ\*,**

where λ\* is the clock depth *at the trigger site*. K has no free coupling. It is the log-slope of a measurable astrophysical distribution, smoothed at a fixed width.

**Two regimes.**

1. *Bulk events* (λ\* ≲ few×10⁻⁵, for example stellar coronae at λ ≈ 2×10⁻⁶). Here ρ_b is dominated by gas and stars spread by large-scale potentials, with rms σ_b ≈ 3–5×10⁻⁵ (a scale-invariant ζ over about 4–5 e-folds of k, times the Λ growth suppression; an estimate). This gives K ≈ −(λ\* − λ̄)/(σ_b² + w²), with s² ≈ 3.7×10⁻⁹. The magnitude is |K| ≲ 10⁴, but **the sign depends on our own large-scale potential λ_LSS,here**, which is uncertain to about 10⁻⁵. Flares and laboratory tests therefore have a derived magnitude but no secure sign.
2. *Compact-trigger events.* SN Ia ignition happens near the centre of a white dwarf of about 1.0–1.4 M_☉ with λ\* ≈ 3×10⁻⁴–10⁻³. Core-collapse bounce happens in an iron core with λ\* ≈ 10⁻³. These sites lie far above the bulk (λ\* ≫ s), so S_w there is set by the **mass of record-forming matter inside massive white dwarfs and pre-collapse cores**. White dwarfs hold roughly 0.5–1 % of baryons (estimate), with λ ≈ 1–2×10⁻⁴ near the 0.6 M_☉ peak. Between 0.6 and about 1.2 M_☉ the white-dwarf mass function falls by about 30× while λ rises by about 4×10⁻⁴. That gives **K_Ia ≈ −ln 30 / 4×10⁻⁴ ≈ −8×10³**, with an uncertainty of about 3× (range −3×10³ to −3×10⁴). K_CC is similar in sign and a few thousand in magnitude. The smoothing at w = 4.6×10⁻⁵ barely affects these values.

**The sign is secure for supernovae.** The trigger depth sits *above* the mode of the partner distribution, so a deeper external potential pushes the trigger into a sparser region of partner space. Supernovae should therefore be **suppressed** in deeper wells.

**Amplitudes for supernovae at fixed host properties** (K = −8×10³):

| External environment | Δλ_ext (estimate) | δ ln Γ |
|---|---|---|
| Satellite in a 10¹³ M_☉ group | ~2×10⁻⁶ | −1.6 % |
| Satellite in a 10¹⁴ M_☉ group | ~8×10⁻⁶ | −6 % |
| Rich-cluster core (σ ≈ 1000 km/s) | ~3×10⁻⁵ | −20 % |
| Large-scale potential variation, z < 0.1 | rms ~5–10×10⁻⁶ | ∓4–8 % |

For comparison, GR time dilation changes observed rates by Δλ itself, about 10⁻⁵. The predicted effect is about 10⁴ times larger and has its own sign structure, so the prediction is non-zero where GR predicts nothing measurable (lesson 6).

**Side predictions** that a generic environmental effect would not produce:

- (i) Rates shift while light-curve properties (stretch, colour, peak luminosity) stay unchanged at fixed progenitor, because links only gate the trigger.
- (ii) K differs between classes in a calculable way. K_Ia and K_CC are both negative. K_flare has magnitude ≲ 10⁴ and its sign is set by λ_LSS,here.
- (iii) K becomes slightly more negative over cosmic time as the massive-white-dwarf tail fills in.

## 5. Dataset, N, 5σ N and kill criterion

**Dataset.** Low-redshift (z < 0.1) supernovae from public, spectroscopically classified, magnitude-limited samples. The baseline is the ZTF Bright Transient Survey (~10⁴ classified SNe as of my knowledge, about 70 % Ia), supplemented by ASAS-SN and TNS classifications, for a total N ≈ 1.5–2×10⁴. Each host gets three measurements:

- the host's stellar mass, sSFR and metallicity, from Legacy Survey/WISE SED fits;
- group or cluster membership with halo mass and projected radius, from public group catalogues built on 2MRS, 6dF and SDSS, plus redMaPPer and eROSITA cluster lists, giving the group potential through an NFW profile;
- the large-scale potential Φ_LSS from public density or peculiar-velocity reconstructions of the local volume, using only the gradient (the monopole and dipole are unconstrained).

The regression variable is Δλ_ext = −(Φ_LSS + Φ_group)/c² (+ v²/2c², which is negligible at the 10⁻⁶ level). To my knowledge, SN rates have been regressed on host stellar mass and on a binary cluster/field split, but **never on external potential depth at fixed host properties**. I have not verified this, because no searches were allowed.

**Statistic.** A Poisson GLM of supernova counts per galaxy, over a mass-complete galaxy census of the same volume:

  ln E[N_SN,g] = ln Γ₀(M\*, sSFR, Z, age) + ln(control time) + K · Δλ_ext,g,

with the covariates modelled flexibly as splines.

**Sensitivity.** The usable spread in Δλ_ext across hosts is σ_Δλ ≈ 5×10⁻⁶ after allowing for reconstruction errors of about 3×10⁻⁶. Those errors dilute the slope by about 0.74, which is included in the numbers below. With σ_K ≈ 1/(0.74 σ_Δλ √N):

- **5σ N** = (5 / (0.74 |K| σ_Δλ))²:
  - **N ≈ 3×10⁴** for the central K = −8×10³;
  - N ≈ 2×10⁵ for K = −3×10³;
  - N ≈ 2×10³ for K = −3×10⁴.
- Cluster cores alone: about 1100 cluster-core SNe for 5σ at δ = −20 %. Current public samples probably hold a few hundred, enough for 2–3σ.
- The current public N of about 2×10⁴ gives ~4σ at the central value. The full LSST era (≥10⁵ hosts with photometric classification) reaches the whole band.

**Kill criterion** (pre-registered):

- **Main kill.** The hypothesis is dead if the 95 % interval for K_Ia lies entirely above −3×10³, the least-negative edge of the derived band. With N = 2×10⁴ this test has σ_K ≈ 1.9×10³, so it has real power now. A positive K_Ia at ≥3σ also kills it.
- **Secondary kills:**
  - (a) a significant Δλ dependence of SN Ia stretch or colour at fixed host properties, together with no rate dependence;
  - (b) K_CC/K_Ia outside the range 0.2–5;
  - (c) a laboratory annual modulation of macroscopic nucleation rates, with CMB-dipole phase, excluded below 10⁻⁵ *once λ_LSS,here is known to ±3×10⁻⁶*.

## 6. Systematics and confounders, and how to separate them

- **Selection.** Detection and classification efficiency is lower on bright, crowded cluster hosts. Fix: inject synthetic transients into the survey's own difference-imaging statistics as a function of host surface brightness. Restrict to the magnitude-limited, classification-complete subset, and model efficiency per host.
- **Age and delay-time distribution.** Satellites are quenched and older, and the SN Ia rate per unit mass falls roughly as t⁻¹. This is the dominant confounder (10–50 %). Separation:
  - (1) Add SED age and sSFR as covariates.
  - (2) Use **phase-space-selected infallers**: galaxies at about R₂₀₀ with high line-of-sight velocity, first infall. These already sit in deep potential, typically 50–70 % of the central depth, but have not been quenched. That breaks the potential–age degeneracy.
  - (3) Use the **large-scale potential term**. Φ_LSS varies on scales of hundreds of Mpc, over which galaxy properties at fixed local density are nearly independent of the potential. Include local density as an explicit covariate, so the potential's long-wavelength part carries the test.
- **Metallicity.** Through the mass–metallicity relation and environmental enrichment, metallicity changes progenitor channels. Use gas-phase metallicity where spectra exist, or the mass–metallicity relation plus its scatter. Metallicity shifts SN *properties* too, whereas the hypothesis predicts rate-only shifts (§4 i).
- **Host mass, IMF and stellar-mass systematics.** Massive ellipticals may have bottom-heavy IMFs, which bias M\* and so the inferred rate per unit mass. Fix: compare at fixed M\* and velocity dispersion. The group-satellite and infaller contrast is at fixed internal properties.
- **Halo-mass and projection errors in Φ_group.** These cause regression dilution, not bias. Model the error explicitly, for example with a SIMEX or errors-in-variables GLM.
- **Distance and redshift.** Mild Malmquist effects and a K-correction are modelled. Time dilation (~10⁻⁵) is negligible.
- **Discriminant summary.** Astrophysical environment effects should (a) track local density and quenching time and not the potential's long-wavelength part, (b) change SN properties along with rates, and (c) carry no fixed ratio between SN classes. The hypothesis predicts the opposite on all three counts.

## 7. Three weakest points

1. **The scope axiom A5 is put in by hand.** If single quantum transitions (radioactive decays) were also gated by links, the annual λ variation would modulate decay rates at about 10⁻⁴–10⁻³. That is at or above existing limits (≲10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴, from memory). The split between "collective runaway" and "quantum transition" has no derivation from A1–A4, so System B should attack it first.
2. **The amplitude chain has two soft links.** First, w = A_s^{1/2} identifies the substrate's matching resolution with the primordial amplitude. This is a postulated unification, and it has a factor-1.7 ambiguity between ζ and Φ. Second, K for supernovae depends on the *trigger-site* clock depth and the massive-white-dwarf and core tail of the partner distribution, so it inherits stellar-structure and mass-function uncertainties of about 3×. The kill threshold uses the band's weak edge, so a true K of about −2×10³ would escape both detection and the kill.
3. **Confounding and non-locality.** The predicted 2–8 % effects sit under astrophysical environmental trends of 10–50 %. Success depends on the infaller and long-wavelength-potential lever arms being truly independent of age and SFR history, which is not guaranteed. Reconstructed Φ_LSS also carries model-dependent errors. Separately, the rate law depends on a non-local, causal-past integral, which permits signalling in principle at about 10⁻⁵⁰. This has no practical effect, but it is conceptually unattractive and makes the model resemble Machian and screened-scalar ideas more than a "reset" would like.
