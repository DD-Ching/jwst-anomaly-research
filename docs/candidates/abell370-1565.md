# Abell 370 1565: background arc candidate oriented across the predicted stretch

## 2026-10-13: vetting record

- **What was flagged.** `lens_consistency.py arcs` on Abell 370 (CANUCS DR1 model and photo-z; D-024 addenda 3–4).
  F200W pipeline source 1565 (39.977168, −1.583366; CANUCS DR1 row 10429, 0.04″) is `anti`: its axis (F150W 150–158°,
  F200W 150–155°, F277W 163–168°) lies about 90° from the predicted stretch (PA 58°). κ is 0.75–0.82 in five
  published models at z 2.8–3.2 and stays below 0.9 up to z = 8, with q_lens ≤ 0.14 and shape-noise P(anti) ≤ 2e-4.
  Earlier tests (moments, the member modelled out, noise axis, deblending, counter-image) are in
  `docs/fields/abell370.md` "Redshift scan of the anti sources".
- **source_uid / run.** `abell370:f200w-1565` from the 2026-10-13 `arcs` run (#198; scratch outputs); scan
  `results/arcs/anti_flip_scan.json` (#199).
- **Score.** None. This is a lens-model consistency flag, not an anomaly-score rank. Predictions are
  `model_prediction`.

**Hypothesis before this session's tests.** 1565 is a z ≈ 2.9 galaxy whose image the models get wrong. Ordinary
alternatives: (a) it is not at z ≈ 2.9 but at or near the cluster redshift, so it is barely lensed and its
orientation is random (P(|Δ| ≥ 60°) = 1/3); (b) a blend or tidal feature; (c) the models miss a local perturber.
What would change the plan: a redshift for 1565 itself, or evidence that the photo-z depends on one band.

**Verdict:** `inconclusive: needs a redshift for 1565 itself`. The z ≈ 2.9 photo-z depends on the F150W/F200W
colour, which the NIRISS F150W point contradicts. 1565 lies inside a z = 0.386 ionised-gas nebula (MUSE), which gives
a concrete ordinary alternative: a cluster-redshift knot, unlensed, with a random orientation. MUSE cannot decide
between the two, because the nebula's [O II] covers 1565's position at the level of the surrounding gas. Status:
`triaged`; not a candidate. No exotic interpretation is considered.

### 2026-10-13 (later): NIRISS route and forced photometry

**Hypothesis before these tests.** If 1565 is z ≈ 2.94, the NIRCam F150W/F200W ratio (0.47) holds in forced
photometry and NIRISS slitless spectra can show [O III]; if the catalogue's NIRCam F150W point is a deblending
artefact, NIRISS and NIRCam F150W agree once both are measured the same way. A detector-row artefact would make the
axis follow the mosaic rows. What would change the plan: an agreed colour at ≥ 3σ either way, or a row-aligned noise axis.

every band; faint row stripes are visible in NIRCam F115W/F150W, but the noise test above shows no row-aligned axis.

**Verdict (unchanged): `inconclusive`.** The z ≈ 2.9 photo-z rests on 2–3σ fluxes, and forced photometry cannot
tell 0.47 from 1.0 in F150W/F200W. The axis is not a row artefact and agrees across two instruments, but a
barely lensed z 0.386 knot (the MUSE nebula) explains it with no anomaly. Public data reach no decision at a
reasonable cost: the only route left is a contamination-modelled custom NIRISS extraction (e.g. grizli,
several hours), whose best case is a 4–5σ [O III] line. Status: `triaged`, parked.
