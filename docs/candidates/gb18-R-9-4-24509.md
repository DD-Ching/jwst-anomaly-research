### The light curve (`observed`, binned by eye on the figures)

The 2006 season sits ~+2,000 counts. In 2007 the flux falls to ~0, then to ~−5,000 counts for most of 2008. It
recovers through 2009 and stays near +500 … −500 counts from 2010 to 2014. The dimming is ~5,000 counts, ~6 % of the
counterpart's RP flux, and lasts ~2 years. It is smooth and flat-bottomed, with no short caustic spikes in the data.

**Verdict:** `known population: slow dimming of a red giant` (`hypothesis` for the cause). The feature is a smooth,
flat-bottomed ~6 % dimming of a red giant lasting ~2 years, with season-to-season baseline changes around it.
Possible causes are long-secondary-period or irregular red-giant variability, or a long eclipse by an extended
occulter. An ordinary slow-dip model with seasonal baseline levels fits as well as or better than any W3 model. The
W3 fit's preference without those levels comes from one season's baseline, not from caustic spikes (none are
sampled). Status: **explained by an ordinary model outside the chain; not a candidate.** Nothing goes to the owner
or outside the repository.

**Chain gap (note only; no change before the field queue finishes):** the chain compares season-offset models only
with single-lens ordinary models. A flat-bottomed slow dip with per-season offsets (k ≈ 13) is the ordinary
alternative this flag needed. Adding it later would be a post-hoc change: record it as such and re-run the
injections.
