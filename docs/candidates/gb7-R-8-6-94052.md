**Verdict:** `known population / processing: short flat dip` (`hypothesis` for the cause). The feature is a ~8-day,
~2,000-count flat-bottomed dip with no sampled caustic spikes. The only spike-like evidence is one night's mean
+460 counts above baseline. An eclipse or occultation of a faint blended star fits as well once that night is
dropped, and so would a short dimming shared with a star 74″ away (a local subtraction systematic is possible but
not shown). Status: **explained; not a candidate.** Nothing goes to the owner or outside the repository.

The verdict rests on the per-night Δχ² evidence above: one night carries the whole preference over an ordinary
flat dip. It does not rest only on the new test.

**Chain change, made after seeing this flag (post hoc; c63a6d5, 2026-10-09):** the night jackknife ranks nights both
against the best single-lens model and against the eclipse model, and keeps the worse ΔBIC. Before, it ranked them
only against the single-lens model, so the nights that carried the preference over the eclipse were never dropped.
